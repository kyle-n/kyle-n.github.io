The New Yorker [profiled](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2026/11/23/the-buddhist-ai-sect-that-wants-to-take-over-silicon-valley) the Monastic Academy for the Preservation of Life on Earth, or MAPLE. The group is "an isolated Buddhist monastery in northern Vermont." They are half Buddhist, half AI doomer and all red flags. Folks, never join a cult that makes you write groveling paeans about its leader. Have some self-respect.

MAPLE is full of kooks who believe AI systems are actually forming collective minds by siphoning off human consciousness. But, the New Yorker writer says:

> In a way, I think the group is closer to the truth than anyone else I'd encountered.
>
> [...]
>
> These new entities really do seem to work by siphoning off human consciousness. Every exciting new capability the machine develops is something we've lost. The smarter it gets, the more paltry our own abilities seem, which is why even people who think that A.I. is the Image of the Beast will still allow the Beast to rearrange their petunias. So much more restful to let it write your e-mails for you, flirt with strangers on dating apps for you, tell bedtime stories to your children for you. Why not let these parts of yourself atrophy? You don't really need them anymore.
>
> All technology is like this. Once, everyone needed to know how to make a fire—not anymore. We have matches and lighters; the knowledge is crystallized in the objects, and you can use them without having any idea how they actually work. We've off-loaded a lot of the knowledge that used to be essential, and as a result most people no longer endure the general misery of growing their own food and spinning their own thread. But, once we start developing machines that can think, we're in uncharted territory. Any use is going to look a little like possession or worship: a surrender of the self.

I find this passage chilling because it's true. I can't grow food or start a fire. Other people figured out how to do those things and more. They encoded that knowledge in tractors and lighters and millions of modern conveniences we use daily.

It just feels _wrong_ now that it's happening to my ability to code, which is something I cherish. I spent years learning to program. Hours and hours painstakingly hand-writing line after line. Huge chunks of my life were spent exercising that muscle, growing it, being _proud_ of it. And now Claude does that stuff in like 30 seconds.

I'm letting that part of myself atrophy. Almost every developer is. AI is so fast at coding, you'd be crazy not to. But it feels weird and scary.
